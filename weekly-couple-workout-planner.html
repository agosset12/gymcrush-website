Put it on the calendar

A Weekly Couple Workout Planner You Can Copy

Write the time you can share first, then give each person their own activity and fallback. Keep open days open. This worksheet organizes your routines without prescribing the same workout to both partners.

Fill in these five decisions

  1. This week's shared priority: time together, a regular check-in, or a specific activity you both enjoy.
  2. Available windows: write the days and times that truly fit around work, travel, and rest.
  3. Individual plans: each person chooses activities from their own routine.
  4. Fallback: decide whether to move the appointment, do separate sessions, or rest.
  5. Review time: pick a short window to organize the following week.

This is an original planning worksheet. The examples are calendar choices, not a complete fitness program or in-app feature. Use familiar activities and appropriate instruction for the workouts themselves.

The rest of the week stays open for individual routines or rest. If three activity appointments are too many, keep one. If your existing plan includes more, add it without requiring your partner to match it.

Use three rules for conflicts

  • A changed schedule gets a new decision. Write “move,” “separate,” or “rest” instead of leaving the other person waiting.
  • No catch-up debt. A missed shared appointment does not mean you owe two sessions tomorrow.
  • Each person owns their plan. Ask before changing the other person's activity, timing, or reminders.

For different time zones, write both local times and the date. For a busy gym, write a suitable alternative before you arrive. For an unpredictable workweek, set a weekly check-in rather than promising an exact daily session.

A five-minute Sunday review

Each answer: “What worked?” “What got in the way?” “What do I want to repeat?” Keep your notes concrete: a slot, a location, or an activity. Choose the next week's appointments only after both calendars are open.

Use a result label such as completed, changed, rested, or missed. The point is to see whether the calendar fits your lives. Avoid turning the worksheet into a comparison of bodies, effort, or who cares more.

When to change the tool

If the note is easy to use, keep it. If you regularly lose track of the agreement, look for a tool that solves that specific issue: reminders, session sharing, or a shared daily ritual. Try one change for a week before adding another.
