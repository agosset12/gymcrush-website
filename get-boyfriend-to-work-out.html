How to Get Your Boyfriend to Work Out (Without Nagging)

It is 9pm. You have shoes on. He is on the couch saying “later,” which, in couch language, could mean next spring. You want a workout buddy and more time together. Start with the second thing.

By Antoine Gosset, founder of GymCrush

Open with a date, not a diagnosis

“I miss doing things together. Want to take a walk after dinner tomorrow or try that Saturday class with me?” is an invitation he can picture. “You really need to exercise” starts a completely different conversation. You may care about health, but a lecture at the door is a terrible date invitation.

Give a real choice and a real time. Tuesday after dinner or Saturday morning beats “we should work out sometime.” If he chooses the walk, book the walk. Do not quietly turn it into your usual hill intervals. If he says no, let that answer stand and keep your own plans; nobody can be dragged into a shared hobby and enjoy it.

Find out what “later” is hiding

“Later” can mean the gym feels intimidating, he is tired, he has no idea what to do, or he simply wants another kind of time together. Ask once: “Is it the timing, the gym, or just not your thing?” Then listen to the answer you get rather than the one you hoped for.

  • “I hate gyms.” Try a walk to a cafe, a beginner dance class, or a living-room playlist. The gym is a place, not the goal.
  • “You are way fitter than I am.” Share the start and finish, with separate routines in between. Walking beside your easy jog is optional; walking a nearby loop is fine.
  • “I do not know what to do.” Choose a simple familiar activity or book an introductory class. You can enjoy the date without becoming his personal trainer.
  • “I never have time.” Put one small slot on the calendar and name what you will move to make room. If the answer is still no, you have useful information.
  • “I will start Monday.” Ask which Monday slot he wants and what activity sounds fun. If he cannot choose one, stop doing the scheduling for both of you.

If he worries about keeping up, try a workout format for two different fitness levels so neither person has to perform for the other.

A first workout date that is easy to say yes to

Choose somewhere familiar and give the date an ending. An after-dinner walk can finish at the corner bakery. A gym visit can mean arriving together, doing your own routines, and meeting by the lockers before getting lunch. A home workout can be two songs of dancing, a familiar guided class, or a short mobility session.

For a new gymgoer, check guest access and class level before you arrive. Bring a backup if the gym is packed. That little bit of planning prevents the classic opening scene: one of you circling the weight room while the other stands by a machine pretending to read a sign.

Keep your first outing short enough that he is still willing to make a second plan. End with “What part would you do again?” If the answer is “the walk there,” great. You have found the next date.

Try a week, then read the room

Here is a script you can steal: “I would love one active date this week. You pick: walk and coffee Wednesday, or a class and lunch Saturday. If neither sounds good, tell me what kind of date you would rather do.” That last sentence matters. Maybe he wants time together and hates workouts; maybe he wants workouts but not as a couple. Both answers are possible.

If he picks Wednesday, put the time and place in the calendar. On the day, a single “Still on for our walk?” is plenty. When the date ends, decide together whether to repeat it, move it, or try something else. If you are the only one making every plan, do your workout anyway and save couple time for a date he will actually join.

The week can be as simple as one active date plus your separate routines. You do not have to announce a new lifestyle, buy matching shoes, or make a spreadsheet that looks like wedding seating. If you both enjoy it, the gym date ideas give you more options.

Make motivation a two-person conversation

Ask what he wants from the outing: company, an easy start, a new place, or a bit of competition. Tell him what you want too. If you want a buddy for your existing hard session and he wants a relaxed date, book those separately. Calling both “working out together” does not make them the same plan.

There are fun ways to share a win without commentary on each other’s bodies. Send a photo after your own workout, pick the next playlist, or agree on a low-stakes bet such as who chooses the movie. A little invitation beats another lecture from the doorway. If he is interested in starting but wants a full exercise plan, use a qualified instructor or an appropriate program rather than guessing together.

And if he never wants to join? Keep working out. Ask for the relationship thing you actually need—more time, more support, more dates—without requiring gym attendance as proof. That is a better conversation than debating his shoes every Tuesday.

Frequently asked questions

What can I do if my partner doesn't want to workout?

Invite him to a specific activity once, ask what puts him off, and accept his answer. Keep your own routine and plan another kind of date if what you really want is time together.

How to motivate your boyfriend at the gym?

Let him choose the activity or part of the visit, arrive with separate plans, and ask what kind of encouragement he likes. A shared start and finish can feel more relaxed than following every set together.

What is the best workout routine for couples?

One you would both repeat: a booked slot, activities you each like, and a quick check-in afterward. Many couples start with three small daily challenges each and one shared streak in GymCrush, so he never has to match your workout.

What are some good fitness ideas for couples?

Try a neighborhood walk, a beginner class, a gym visit with separate routines, a two-song dance date, or a shared activity at different times followed by a photo check-in.
