Start with an invitation

How to Invite Your Boyfriend to Work Out With You

Ask whether he wants a shared activity, offer two specific choices, and let him say no. You can plan an invitation; you cannot make the decision for him.

Published October 8, 2026

On this page

  • Ask about the activity, not his body
  • Make the invitation easy to answer
  • Try a one-week agreement
  • Use a shared agreement, not a running argument
  • If he still does not want to join

Ask about the activity, not his body

Start with the time you want to spend together. Try: “I'd like us to do something active together this week. Would you be up for a short walk after dinner or a gym visit Saturday?” This is a conversation prompt, not a technique guaranteed to change his motivation.

If he says no, ask whether another activity would interest him, once. You can still do your own workout. Avoid making affection, dinner, or approval depend on whether he joins you.

Make the invitation easy to answer

Common objections and an optional next step
What he saysWhat you could offer
“I have no time.”“Would a short walk before dinner fit, or should we leave it this week?”
“I do not like the gym.”“Would dancing at home or an outdoor walk be more your thing?”
“You are much fitter than me.”“We can book the same time and do different activities. We do not need matching numbers.”
“I do not know what to do.”“We could book a gym orientation, or choose something you already know.”
“Reminders annoy me.”“Would you prefer one agreed reminder or none?”
“Maybe later.”“Do you want to pick a day now, or should I make my own plan?”

Try a one-week agreement

  1. Choose one shared slot. Write an actual day, place, and finish time. “Saturday after breakfast, around the neighborhood, home by 10” is easier to act on than “work out more.”
  2. Let him choose something. The activity, route, music, or start time can be his choice. Neither partner needs to become the coach.
  3. Pick a fallback. If the gym is crowded, take the agreed walk. If one person is unavailable, each can use the slot independently.
  4. Agree on reminders. Write the wording and timing together. A suggested message is “Still on for our plan?”
  5. Review once. After the week, ask “Was there anything you liked enough to repeat?” Book another slot only if both want to.

Use a shared agreement, not a running argument

Copy and complete: “This week we want to try ____. Our time is ____. My role is ____. Your role is ____. We want a reminder at ____, or none. If the plan changes, we will ____. On Sunday we will decide whether to repeat it.”

Keep each person's reason separate. You may want more movement; he may want time outside. There is no need to manufacture the same goal. Ask before giving exercise feedback, taking a photo, or sharing anything with other people.

If he still does not want to join

Leave the invitation open without repeating it every day. Keep your own routine or find a willing friend for your sessions. If the real issue is wanting more time together, say that directly and choose another kind of date. A fitness app is useful only if both people want the arrangement.
