Most habit trackers are a private list of things you feel bad about. This one is three small things a day, a person who notices when you do them, and two cartoon animals who are very invested in your progress.

Pick three. That is it.

Each of you picks three habits a day from a wall of them. Workouts like push-ups, squats, a plank, or a run. Gentle ones like stretching, yoga, or a walk. And the ones that are not workouts at all: drink water, morning sunlight, eight hours of sleep, no phone before bed.

Your three do not have to match theirs. One of you can do burpees while the other meditates. Both count. For more ideas, see these couple workout challenges and fitness goals for couples.

Why habits stick better with two

Someone notices

When you finish, your partner’s phone lights up. When they finish, yours does. That tiny “your turn” does more than any reminder you set for yourself.

And because you share one streak, quitting quietly stops being an option.

What to look for in a couples habit tracker

  • One shared streak, not two lists side by side.
  • Different habits for each partner, so your levels do not have to match.
  • A nudge when your partner finishes.
  • Forgiveness for bad days, so one miss does not wipe out a month.
  • A reason to open it that is not guilt.
