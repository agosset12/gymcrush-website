Best Couple Workout Apps: Choose by How You Train

The best couple workout app depends on what you want to share. Compare a workout log, a group challenge, and guided classes before deciding whether you need a dedicated daily couples ritual.

Hevy: exchanging routines and workout logs

Hevy lets you share a planned routine or a completed workout, including with someone who does not use the app. Its guide distinguishes a reusable plan from a finished session. Standard weights and repetitions from the sender are not transferred with a shared routine; the recipient's own history supplies those values.

Before choosing it for two people, exchange a sample routine and a completed workout. Check that each person can see the information they actually need. Read Hevy’s sharing instructions.

Strava: a shared activity goal

Strava's mobile Group Challenges can include a Group Goal based on time, distance, elevation gain, or elevation loss. Participants' activities contribute to that goal. Challenges are private to participants. Both creation and participation require a subscription or free trial, according to Strava's current guide.

Before setting a target, check which activities and visibility settings qualify. Choose a target both people want to contribute to, rather than assuming equal distances. Read Strava’s Group Challenge requirements.

Apple Fitness+: choosing guided sessions

Apple Fitness+ includes guided strength, yoga, dance, and other workout types. You can filter sessions by duration and build a custom plan. Apple also documents Family Sharing for up to five other family members, with an individual experience for each person.

If you plan to follow a session together, check the device setup, equipment, space, and suitability for each person before choosing the class. Sharing access and choosing an appropriate session are separate decisions. Read Apple’s Fitness+ feature and device guide.

Make a choice with five questions

  1. Do we want instruction, a detailed exercise log, a collective target, or a daily completion check?
  2. Can each person access the features we need on their own device?
  3. What information becomes visible, and to whom?
  4. Which shared features require a subscription or trial for each person?
  5. After a sample session, do both people want to use this setup again?

If none of those app features is necessary, start with a shared calendar appointment and a completion message. You can add software after identifying a problem it should solve.
