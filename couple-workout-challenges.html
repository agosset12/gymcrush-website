18 Couple Workout Challenges to Try Together

Pick one activity you both want to try, agree on separate targets, and choose when to check in. A couple challenge can be as simple as taking a walk and telling each other you finished.

Set the rules before you start

Use one shared theme and let each person choose the amount they can comfortably do. Count participation instead of declaring whoever does more the winner. Agree that either person can choose rest or change the activity. Keep any reward small and optional, such as choosing the next movie.

The activity names below come from our app's challenge catalog. The pair formats are original suggestions for this guide; they are not preset app programs. Choose activities you already know how to do, or get instruction before trying an unfamiliar movement.

Six challenges for getting moving

  1. Walking: choose an out-and-back route and a turnaround time. If you are apart, send one detail you noticed outside afterward.
  2. Running / jogging: each person chooses their own pace and route. Start or finish at an agreed meeting point; compare the experience instead of the distance.
  3. Dancing: each partner picks one song. Clear enough floor space for both of you and take turns choosing the next track.
  4. Jump rope: take separate turns with enough clearance. Agree on your own short attempt before starting; watching and encouraging counts as the partner role.
  5. Jumping jacks: choose your own number and stop when you reach it. If this movement does not suit either person, swap the shared theme to walking.
  6. High knees: take turns doing a familiar version while the other keeps time. Neither partner sets the other person's pace.

Six strength challenge choices

  1. Push-ups: each person uses a variation they already practice. Record your own completed amount, with no cross-partner ranking.
  2. Squats: choose your own familiar setup. Take turns so there is no rush to keep up with someone else's repetitions.
  3. Lunges: give each person clear floor space and a separate target. Replace the exercise if it is uncomfortable rather than chasing a shared number.
  4. Glute bridges: put down separate mats. Decide the amount individually, then check in when you are both finished.
  5. Calf raises: use your own usual setup and target. Each partner records completion in a shared note.
  6. Plank: choose your own hold duration in advance. Finish at that point instead of turning the activity into a last-person-standing contest.

Six quieter choices

  1. Stretching: each person chooses familiar stretches. Keep your own range of motion; do not pull or push your partner into position.
  2. Yoga flow: select a familiar sequence or a guided class that suits you. Different versions can count toward the same shared theme.
  3. Hip openers: each partner chooses movements they already know. You can replace this with another familiar mobility activity.
  4. Breathing session: choose a quiet moment and a comfortable, familiar practice. Treat it as an optional check-in ritual.
  5. Meditation: agree on a short window to sit quietly, together or separately. Share whether you want to repeat it, with no score for doing it correctly.
  6. Phone-free before bed: agree where phones will go and when the window starts. This is a shared habit option, not an exercise session.

Turn your choices into one week

For example: choose walking on Monday, your familiar strength routine on Wednesday, and a two-song dance date on Saturday. On Sunday, each pick one activity to repeat. Leave the other days open for your own routines or rest. The point of this example is to book time, not prescribe a complete fitness program.

Keep a simple record

Copy this line into a note: “Date / shared theme / my target / your target / done, changed, or rested / what to repeat.” If a target was too much, change the next attempt. A skipped day does not require a catch-up contest.
